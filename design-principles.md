# Design principles

## The triad

**Black, white and BINDER Red** produce the primary BINDER colour impression.
This triad is the core of the appearance and a primary design element in its
own right. Everything else — the grays, the red shades, the chart colours —
supports it and must never dilute its contrast.

- **White** is used generously and over large areas: for structure, as
  background, as white space, and as the design colour in negative
  applications. White is what gives the triad its contrast.
- **Black** is the primary text colour and the base for graphics and icons.
  Avoid large, full-coverage black areas.
- **BINDER Red** is the highlight: emphasis in type, graphic elements, icons,
  and elements that need to stand out.

## Brand characteristics and what they mean for design

| The brand is | So the design is |
|---|---|
| Orderly, structured, down-to-earth | Uncluttered. Clear information hierarchy. Plenty of white space. |
| Simple, plain, factual, rational | High contrast — large against small, colour against neutral. Fact-driven, to the point. No decorative elements, no unusual styling. |
| Confident, assertive, precise | Deliberate visual guidance. Considered typographic contrast, e.g. large figures against standard sizes. |
| Approachable, personal, credible | For corporate and service content: authentic imagery, light colouring, quotation layers. |
| Technological | For product information: tables, charts, diagrams, lists, short precise text, product photography and details. |
| Dynamic, innovative | Saturated, high-contrast colour in the black/white/red triad. |
| Exclusive, unique | Plenty of white space, high-grade materials and print, print finishing where possible. Avoid digital printing where you can. Discreet, restrained logo placement. |

## Formal language

- Straight construction from **horizontal and vertical** forms
- **Circular elements** as high-contrast accents
- **No organic shapes**
- **No large diagonal areas or lines**
- Clear separation between conveying information and brand communication
- Intuitive navigation, focused on usability
- Clear, uncluttered layout with detail-oriented execution

## The red line above titles

A short horizontal red line sits directly above main titles. It is a discreet,
distinctive element that ties visual communication together — advertisements,
banners, documents.

All three measurements derive from the font size, so the rule holds at any
scale:

| Property | Rule |
|---|---|
| Colour | BINDER Red |
| Placement | Directly above the main title, always starting flush left |
| Thickness | **0.10 ×** font size |
| Length | The width of the **first three letters** of the title |
| Distance to title | **0.3 ×** font size |

**Worked example** — at 50 pt type: line thickness 5 pt, distance to the title
16.6 pt. At 25 pt type: thickness 2.5 pt, distance 8.3 pt.
